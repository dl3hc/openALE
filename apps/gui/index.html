Getting Started
Quick Start

openALE is a software console for MIL-STD-188-141B 2nd-Generation Automatic Link Establishment (2G ALE). It talks to a radio through Hamlib/CAT and to the operator through this browser page. Follow the steps below to get a fresh install talking on the air.

Mobile shortcut: on the mobile layout, open the ☰ More menu on first run — a 4-step Setup Wizard walks through Callsign, Audio, Radio/CAT and Channels automatically. The steps below cover the same ground manually and apply to both layouts.
  1. Set your station callsign.

    Open Settings → Identity and add a Self Address (your station's ALE callsign, letters/digits, no spaces). This is broadcast as SELF in the header and is what other stations call to reach you.

  2. Connect your radio.

    Open Settings → Radio / CAT, pick your Hamlib rig model, fill in the network host/port or serial port, then click Connect. Use Test Connection first if you just want to confirm reachability without taking control of the radio.

  3. Pick your audio devices.

    Open Settings → Audio and select the RX Input (from the radio) and TX Output (to the radio) devices. Run the level meter to confirm audio is actually flowing before you rely on scanning.

  4. Load a channel plan.

    Open Settings → Files and load a .ale channel file — the nets/ folder ships with ready-made HFLINK presets (hflink_usa.ale, hflink_eur.ale, hflink_uk.ale, hflink_ja.ale, hflink_aus.ale). This populates the Channels and Nets tabs.

  5. Review Channels and Nets.

    Settings → Channels lists every frequency openALE will scan; Settings → Nets groups channels together with a dwell/sounding schedule. Adjust or add entries for your own frequency plan if the presets don't fit.

  6. Save & Apply.

    Click Save & Apply at the bottom of Settings to push everything to the running bridge. See the Files section for what is and isn't remembered between restarts.

  7. Start scanning.

    Close Settings and click the Scan toggle in the header. openALE will step through your channel plan, sound periodically, and listen for calls.

  8. Make a call.

    Pick a destination in the Net dropdown or type an address directly, then use the call control in the header to initiate a handshake. Linked stations appear with a LINKED status and an active channel.

New to ALE terminology? Check the Glossary tab for plain-language definitions of SELF, LQA, SINAD, Sounding and more.

Glossary

Plain-language definitions for the ALE and radio jargon used throughout openALE.

SELF
Your station's own ALE address (callsign), shown in the header. A station may have more than one Self Address, each restricted to particular channels if needed.
Net
A named group of channels sharing one dwell/sounding schedule and a common calling list — the unit you actually "join" and scan against.
Channel
One RX/TX frequency pair (with mode) that ALE can scan, sound and call on. Channels can be restricted to receive-only, transmit-only, or excluded from calling/sounding.
Scanning
Continuously stepping through a channel list listening for calls addressed to you or to AllCall.
Sounding
A station periodically transmitting an unaddressed test signal so other stations can measure the link quality to it on that channel, without a full handshake.
LQA (Link Quality Analysis)
The database of measured link quality (BER/SINAD-derived scores) between stations and channels, built up from soundings and handshakes. Used to automatically pick the best channel when calling.
SINAD
Signal to Noise-And-Distortion ratio, in dB — one of the raw measurements behind an LQA score. Higher is better.
BER
Bit Error Rate measured from the ALE waveform's redundant (Golay) encoding — the other raw measurement behind an LQA score. Lower is better.
Golay(24,12) / FEC votes
The forward error correction code ALE words are encoded with. Each bit is sent with redundancy; the demodulator "votes" across repeats to correct errors — see the FEC/Sync section.
Handshake
The 3-way call/response/acknowledge exchange that establishes (links) a connection between two stations on a chosen channel.
AMD (Automatic Message Display)
A short free-text data message (up to ~90 characters) sent over an established or unlinked ALE channel — the closest thing ALE has to a text message.
AllCall
The reserved broadcast address every ALE station listens to in addition to its own — used for calls to "anyone listening."
Hamlib / CAT
Computer Aided Transceiver control — the library and protocol openALE uses to remote-control your radio's frequency, mode and PTT over serial or network.
PTT Lead / Tail
Extra time openALE keys the radio before audio starts (lead) and keeps it keyed after audio ends (tail), to absorb relay/amplifier turn-on latency.
Dwell Time
How long a scan pauses on each channel while listening, before moving to the next.
Grid Locator (Maidenhead)
A compact alphanumeric geographic coordinate (e.g. EM12) used as an alternative to decimal lat/lon in the Location tab.
SFI (Solar Flux Index)
A measure of solar activity that influences HF propagation — optionally factored into LQA channel scoring.
Relink
Automatically re-establishing a link on a better channel when the current one's measured quality drops below a threshold.
Enhanced Frequency Select
Using the LQA database (instead of just scan order) to choose the best-scoring channel when placing a call.
LBT (Listen Before Transmit)
A busy-channel check performed immediately before transmitting, to avoid keying up over another station.
Station
Identity

Defines who this station is on the air. Every Self Address here is one you can be called on.

Self Addresses table

Lists every ALE address this station answers to, its enabled/disabled status, and which channels it is valid on (leave blank for "all channels"). The header's SELF readout shows the first enabled address. Use + Add Self Address to add another — useful for a station that answers to more than one callsign or role address.

Station Name

A free-text label for your own reference (e.g. site name). It is not transmitted over ALE and does not need to be unique.

Station
Radio / CAT

Controls how openALE remote-controls your transceiver via Hamlib.

Rig Model

Select your transceiver from the Hamlib model list. This determines which CAT command set is used.

Connection type — Network vs Serial

Network rigs (e.g. rigctld, IP-connected transceivers) need Host and Port. Serial rigs need a Port name plus Baud Rate and, for some models, DTR/RTS line settings and a stabilization delay after opening the port.

PTT Method

Chooses how transmit is keyed: via the CAT command set itself, via a separate serial control line, or via an external PTT port. Use PTT Port to specify that separate line when it isn't CAT.

TX Audio Input

On rigs that accept audio over the same CAT/USB link (rather than a sound-card cable), selects which input the transmit audio is injected into.

Avoid Relay Click While Scanning

Puts the rig in Hamlib SPLIT mode while receiving/scanning so the PA's band/lowpass-filter relays don't click on every scan hop; SPLIT is dropped automatically right before transmit or sounding. No effect on rigs whose Hamlib backend doesn't support split VFO control.

CAT / Rig Traffic trace

An opt-in diagnostic toggle that prints every raw CAT command/response as "[CAT] ..." lines in the ALE Log — useful when a rig isn't responding as expected.

Test Connection vs Connect

Test Connection opens the port/socket just long enough to confirm the rig answers, then releases it. Connect takes and holds control of the radio for actual operation — do this before scanning or calling.

Station
Tuner

An optional read-only netrigctl server, for feeding your current frequency to an external auto-tuner or other Hamlib-aware tool without giving it control of the radio.

netrigctl Server

Serves the current frequency (read-only) over the Hamlib netrigctl protocol. Does not affect openALE's own connection to the radio.

Port / Network Access

The TCP port the server listens on, and whether it's reachable only from this machine or from the whole LAN.

Apply

Starts or restarts the server with the current settings.

Station
Location & Propagation

Your station's geographic position — used to improve LQA-based channel scoring against propagation conditions, and as the source for ALE-GPR position reports.

Position Source

Choose None (position unused), manual Lat/Lon, a Maidenhead Grid Locator, or a live GPS feed (gpsd or a serial NMEA source).

Use SFI in scoring

When enabled, factors the current Solar Flux Index (daily 10.7 cm solar flux from NOAA SWPC) into LQA channel scoring — channels measured under similar solar/ionospheric conditions get a scoring bonus.

Position Reports

Off by default. Sends an ALE-GPR (or raw-GGA) position report as an AMD message, using the live GPS fix when available or the configured station position otherwise.

Enable Position Reports

Master switch gating both automatic and manual "Send Position" sends.

Destination — Direct Address / ALLCALL

Send to one station, or broadcast to everyone listening. ALLCALL is a one-shot broadcast — no reply is expected or waited for.

Automatic Reporting — On Change / Interval

On Change fires whenever you've moved past the configured distance threshold (a 60 s minimum always applies, regardless of threshold); Interval fires on a fixed timer.

Format — ALE-GPR / Raw NMEA GGA

Raw GGA passthrough needs a live NMEA-serial GPS fix; it's unavailable for gpsd, manual, or grid-derived positions.

Standing Comment

Used verbatim by automatic (on-change/interval) sends. The manual "Send Position Now" comment pre-fills from this but can be overridden per send.

Location Relay

Off by default. Forwards position reports received from other stations to an external HTTPS endpoint — for live mapping outside openALE. Your own transmitted positions are never forwarded.

API Endpoint / Relay Identity / CA Certificate Path

The relay server's URL, this station's auto-generated Ed25519 identity (callsign + public key — self-registers with the server, then needs one-time operator approval), and (for a self-signed certificate) the path to the server's certificate for pinning.

Forward positions received via

Choose which call types' position reports get forwarded — ALLCALL, Individual, Net, Group, and/or an established Linked session.

Position rounding

Default 6 = full precision. Lower to trade position accuracy for privacy (2 digits ≈ 1 km); also stabilizes deduplication of repeated reports.

Apply

Pushes the location settings to the running bridge immediately, without waiting for the modal's Save & Apply.

ALE
Channels

The list of individual RX/TX frequency pairs openALE can scan, sound, and call on.

ID

A short label identifying the channel (shown in the frequency display and channel steppers in the header).

RX / TX Frequency & Mode

The receive and transmit frequencies (simplex channels use the same value for both) and the ALE waveform mode used on it.

Direction flags

Mark a channel rx_only or tx_only to exclude it from the half it shouldn't be used for — e.g. a monitor-only frequency.

Inhibit flags

Independently exclude a channel from calling, sounding, or reporting/logging, while still leaving it available for the other operations.

ALE-only / short LBT

Marks a channel as ALE-only, applying a shorter Listen-Before-Transmit window appropriate for a channel with no voice traffic to avoid.

+ Add Channel

Appends a new blank channel row to configure. Remember to save — see the Files section for how Channels are persisted.

ALE
Nets

A Net groups one or more Channels under a shared scanning/sounding schedule and a common name shown in the header's Net picker.

Automatic Sounding

Enable periodic multi-channel sounding on the active net. Each net's Sounding Interval (below) sets how often its channels are sounded, keeping the LQA database current for automatic channel selection.

Dwell Time

How long the scanner listens on each member channel per pass before moving on.

Sounding Interval

How often this net's channels are sounded, in minutes — keeps the LQA database current for automatic channel selection.

Calling Length override

Overrides the global calling-segment duration (Timing tab) for this net specifically, useful for nets with unusually congested or clean channels.

+ Add Net

Creates a new net; assign existing channels to it from the Channels tab or when editing the net.

ALE
Timing

Protocol timing parameters, mostly aligned to MIL-STD-188-141B defaults. Change these only if you understand the handshake state machine — values that are too tight will cause missed handshakes with other implementations.

Call Timeout

How long to wait for a response after placing a call before giving up.

Handshake Timeout

Maximum time allowed for the full call/response/acknowledge exchange to complete.

Link Idle Timeout

How long an established link may sit idle (no data/AMD/voice) before it is automatically torn down.

AMD Wait

Delay before considering an AMD send complete, allowing time for the far end to acknowledge.

Sounding Warning Lead

How far in advance of a scheduled sounding to log/flag it, useful when coordinating manual operation around automatic soundings.

Turn-Around Time

The gap left between transmit and receive direction changes during a handshake, to allow radio and relay switching to settle.

Max Tune Time

Upper bound on how long an antenna tuner is given to complete a tune cycle before ALE proceeds regardless.

PTT Lead / PTT Tail

Extra key-up time before audio starts and extra key-down time after audio ends, to absorb amplifier/relay latency. Keep Lead as low as your hardware allows — it eats into the ~650 ms minimum protocol reply window. SDR/virtual-audio-cable setups typically need a higher Tail (150–250 ms) since that pipeline's buffering is invisible to openALE; raising Tail is always safe, as this station's own reply windows extend to match.

ALE
Policy

Controls how incoming calls are accepted.

Auto-Accept

When on, incoming calls that pass the filters below link automatically. When off, every call requires manual acceptance within the call-timeout window.

Accept Any Call

Accepts calls from stations not in any known contact/roster list, not just recognized ones.

Accept AllCall

Accepts broadcast calls to the AllCall address in addition to calls addressed specifically to one of your Self Addresses.

Accept AMD

Accepts inbound AMD text messages automatically rather than requiring a linked session first.

Link Policy (Outbound Defaults)

The TX-side counterpart of Inbound Acceptance above — what this station offers by default when initiating a call.

Individual / Group / ALLCALL

Default TIS/TWAS conclusion for a fresh AMD send of each call type: TIS keeps the link open, TWAS delivers the message and lets the handshake conclude. The compose-row Link checkbox is pre-filled from this and is always overridable per send.

ALE
LQA

Link Quality Analysis — the measured-quality database that lets openALE automatically pick the best channel to call a station on.

Record LQA

When on, every sounding and handshake updates the LQA database with a fresh score for that station/channel pair.

Auto-Relink + threshold

Automatically re-links an established call onto a better-scoring channel once the current channel's LQA score drops below the configured threshold.

Enhanced Frequency-Select

Uses LQA scores (rather than plain scan order) to choose which channel to call a station on.

Request LQA

Actively asks a station to exchange LQA data rather than waiting for a passive sounding.

LQA Age Limit

Scores older than this are treated as stale and excluded from automatic channel selection.

LQA table (FROM / TO / Score / TIS / TWAS)

FROM and TO identify the station pair; Score is the derived quality figure; TIS ("this is") / TWAS ("that was") show the most recent vs. previous measurement for that pair/channel. Use Refresh to re-pull from the bridge, Clear to wipe the database.

Modem
Audio Devices

Two distinct audio interfaces, configured on two potentially different machines: the Modem Audio Interface is the transceiver's own sound-card link, set up on whichever machine runs the controller/bridge. The Operator Audio Interface is this browser's own mic/speaker, saved locally and never sent to the controller — it may or may not be the same machine.

Modem Audio Interface
RX Input / TX Output

Select the audio interface connected to your radio's receive and transmit audio. On a typical setup this is a USB sound-card or interface, not your computer's built-in mic/speakers.

Level meter

Shows live input level once a device is selected — use it to confirm the radio's audio is actually reaching openALE before trusting scan/decode results.

TX Volume

Adjusts transmit audio output level — set with your radio's ALC in mind; too hot causes distortion the far station will fail to decode.

Connect Audio / Level Test / Refresh List

Connect Audio opens the selected devices for use. Level Test runs a short local loopback/level check. Refresh List re-scans the system for newly attached devices.

Operator Audio Interface

Carries analog voice passthrough, the channel monitor, and audible notifications — all on this browser's own device.

Microphone / Speaker device

Select the browser input/output devices used for voice once linked. Click Refresh Devices if a newly-plugged device isn't listed.

Voice Passthrough — Armed

When on, voice passthrough activates automatically as soon as a link is established, rather than requiring a manual step.

Mic Test

Plays back your microphone locally so you can confirm levels before going on the air.

Channel Monitor

A "listen in" toggle in the main header (next to Sync / FREQ CLEAR) streams the transceiver's RX audio here regardless of link state — useful for listening to the channel while scanning or idle.

Notifications — Ring / Chime / Volume

Optional audible tones played through the selected speaker device: a ring while a call is incoming, and a chime when a message arrives. Both are synthesized locally, so no bundled sound files are needed.

Modem
FEC / Sync

Controls the Golay(24,12) forward-error-correction voting used to recover ALE words from noisy audio.

Correction mode (3/4, 2/5, 1/6, 0/7)

Each ALE word is sent with 7-fold redundancy; these modes trade how many of the 7 repeats may disagree (be "corrected") before a word is accepted versus rejected as unrecoverable. More correction tolerance decodes weaker signals but risks accepting a wrong word.

Min Unanimous Votes

Out of the 49 possible bit votes per word, the minimum that must agree for a bit to be trusted outright before Golay correction is applied.

Adaptive FEC

When on, openALE adjusts correction strictness automatically based on recent channel conditions instead of using a fixed mode.

Advanced
Logging

Controls how much detail openALE writes to its log file and the on-screen ALE Log panel.

Log Level

From least to most verbose. Raise this when diagnosing a problem; leave it low for normal operation to keep the log readable.

Log File

The path logs are written to on disk, in addition to the in-app ALE Log panel.

RX Debug Output

Dumps additional raw demodulator diagnostics — intended for troubleshooting decode issues, not routine use.

Advanced
Files

Three independent files back different parts of openALE's configuration. They are not all handled the same way — read this section carefully.

Configuration File (ale.conf) — Export / Import

A portable snapshot of settings: timing, policy, LQA toggles, self addresses, audio device names. Nothing loads this automatically — click Export to save a snapshot, Import to restore one.

Channel File (.ale) — Load / Save

Holds your Channels and Nets.

Important: this file is not loaded automatically at startup, and editing Channels/Nets and clicking Save & Apply does not write this file. After adding or changing channels/nets, you must explicitly click Save here to keep them, and explicitly Load this file again the next time you start openALE — otherwise your channel plan will be gone on restart.
LQA Database (lqa.bin)

The one file that genuinely takes care of itself: it auto-loads at startup and auto-saves on shutdown (see also Misc → Auto-save LQA on Exit). No manual action is needed for LQA history to persist.

Advanced
Misc / Sounding

Miscellaneous behavior that doesn't fit elsewhere: channel-busy detection and scan squelch calibration.

Auto-save LQA on Exit

When on, the LQA database is written to lqa.bin automatically on shutdown, in addition to its automatic load at startup.

LBT Busy Detection + margin

Blocks TX when channel energy exceeds the tracked noise floor by the margin. Raise the margin at noisy locations.

LBT Override

Emergency use: transmit even when the channel appears occupied. Use with care, as this can step on another station.

Scan Stop Squelch + margin / Calibrate

Sets the signal threshold that pauses the scanner on a channel with activity. Click Calibrate to have openALE sample the current noise floor and suggest a margin automatically.

Help
Troubleshooting

Common first-run and operational issues.

"Bridge Offline" flashes briefly on load

Normal — the page is reconnecting its WebSocket to the bridge. It should clear within a few seconds if openALE is actually running; if it doesn't clear, confirm the bridge process is up and reachable on this port.

Radio / Scan controls are greyed out

Radio/CAT isn't connected yet. Go to Settings → Radio / CAT and click Connect — Test Connection alone only checks reachability, it doesn't take control of the rig.

No stations ever appear in Heard Stations

Confirm audio is actually arriving (Settings → Audio → run the level meter), that a net/channel is selected, and that Scan is running in the header.

Channels/Nets are gone after restarting openALE

Expected with the current persistence behavior — see the Files section. Load your .ale file explicitly each session, or re-export/import it as needed.

Test Channel shows failures on every channel

The remote peer must actually be reachable on each tested frequency — real propagation and timing (band conditions, time of day) affect the result just as they would a live sounding.
